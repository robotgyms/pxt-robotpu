# Robot PU — The Playful, Programmable micro:bit Robot with an ESP32-S3 Smart Hat

**Robot PU** is a complete, classroom-ready robot built on the BBC micro:bit V2. Add the optional **ESP32-S3 Smart Hat** (sold separately) to unlock AI-camera perception, robot soccer, and SLAM-style navigation. Robot PU walks, talks, dances, plays soccer, explores autonomously, and runs real SLAM — all programmable with MakeCode blocks, JavaScript/TypeScript, or Python. From elementary classrooms to college robotics labs, Robot PU turns abstract STEM concepts into hands-on, creative projects.

![Robot PU](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/robotgyms/pxt-robotpu/main/assets/robotpu.png)

## What makes Robot PU special

- **All-in-one robot kit** — pre-built body, servos, sensors, and a second micro:bit gamepad included.
- **Three coding paths** — drag-and-drop MakeCode blocks, typed JavaScript/TypeScript, and a Python package.
- **Dual-brain architecture** — micro:bit handles motion, sensors, and radio; the optional ESP32-S3 Smart Hat powers computer vision, SLAM, and soccer autonomy.
- **60+ learn-then-create projects** — structured lessons from first steps to advanced autonomy.
- **Open-source extension** — free tutorials, example code, and a growing community.

## Fun and advanced features

### Walk, dance, and express
- **Walk, turn, side-step, kick, jump, and rest** with high-level action APIs.
- **Dance routines** that react to music beats and emotion states.
- **Talk and sing** with melodic speech, RoboVoice, and custom song generation.
- **Blinking LED eyes** that show mood, alerts, and sensor feedback.

### Autopilot and navigation
- **Autopilot exploration** with sonar obstacle avoidance.
- **Maze solving** and **path following** for guided missions.
- **Local planner** that picks the safest direction on the fly.
- **Leader following** and **decision engine** for coordinated movement.

### Soccer and SLAM (powered by the ESP32-S3 Smart Hat)
- **Robot soccer ball tracking** and field perception using the AI camera on the Smart Hat.
- **SLAM-style odometry** and **occupancy-grid mapping** offloaded to the ESP32-S3.
- **Shared map** experiments for multi-robot collaboration.
- **2D mapping concepts** that bridge to college-level robotics.

### Communication and multi-robot systems
- **Radio gamepad** and **peer-to-peer chat** between robots.
- **Synchronized singing** and **formation control** for robot teams.
- **Task allocation** and **behavior trees** for complex missions.
- **ROS-inspired concepts** — nodes, topics, telemetry, diagnostics, and safety watchdogs.

### Sensing and control
- **Sonar distance**, **IMU tilt/roll/pitch**, **compass heading**, and **microphone/music** input.
- **Signal filtering**, **heading fusion**, and **PID balance** control.
- **Servo trim calibration**, **feedback loops**, and **kinematics** exploration.

## 60+ classes — from elementary to college

Robot PU is designed as a continuous learning platform. The tutorials are organized into beginner, intermediate, and advanced tracks, with difficulty ratings from one to three stars.

### Ages 8–10 (Elementary) — Beginner
Students start with blocks and simple sequences:
- Project skeleton and hello-world demos
- How Robot PU moves
- Robot actions, dance, and music
- Remote control and gamepad patterns
- Adding arms and calibrating servos

### Ages 11–13 (Middle school) — Intermediate
Students move into JavaScript/TypeScript, events, and autonomy:
- Variables, loops, arrays, and custom events
- State machines and object-oriented design
- Obstacle avoidance, maze solving, and autopilot
- ROS-style nodes, topics, and telemetry
- Peer-to-peer radio and synchronized behavior

### Ages 14–17 (High school / early college) — Advanced
Students tackle real robotics and AI concepts:
- SLAM odometry, occupancy grids, and path planning
- AI camera perception, face detection, and object tracking
- PID control, balance, heading fusion, and signal filters
- Behavior trees, mission recipes, and multi-robot coordination
- Task allocation, formation control, and fault recovery

The full tutorial index is maintained in the [tutorials/README.md](tutorials/README.md) and includes knowledge graphs that show how each topic connects.
